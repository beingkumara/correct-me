Correct Me blueprint

Three pieces: a plain page, a small server that keeps the key, and your rules written as code. The AI only judges lines. The code enforces everything your PRD promises.

The page

Plain HTML/CSS/JS. Shrinks the photo, shows lines and marks, and keeps your session in memory so a failure never loses work.

The server

Node + Express on your laptop. Holds the Groq key in .env. Two routes: /api/read and /api/check.

Your rules, as code

Validate every reply, guard against leaks, apply first-wrong and unsure rules, and strip the private answer before anything leaves.

"The prompt can't forget a rule if the rule isn't in the prompt."

What it checks

  1. Fragments: the corrected line, its right-hand side, the final answer, and its right-hand side. Each comes as written and without "+ C".
  2. Normalize both sides: lowercase, no spaces, · × ∙ ⋅ * removed (so x·sin x = x sin x), − – — ‐ - → -, trailing +c removed.
  3. Hit: a fragment appears inside the note or hint.
  4. Skip: fragments under 3 characters (like x), which are logged as skipped_short.
  5. On a hit: a generic note and hint are shown, plus one JSON line in logs/leak-guard.jsonl with exactly what was caught.

Accepted limit: a reworded leak ("the antiderivative of sin is negative cosine") gets past string matching. The logs tell you how often the model even tries.

Try: "It should be − cos x" (caught) · "x sin x + cos x" (caught) · "What's the derivative of cos x?" (passes)

The useful unknown

Can free open models actually do this job? Read your handwriting, land on line 5, and say "unsure" honestly? That's your evaluating-AI-output goal, tested on your own app.

First build step: npm run reality-check prints the raw model replies next to the final result. You need to see:

  1. All 6 lines read correctly, or the misreads marked "?"
  2. Line 5 flagged, with a hint that doesn't give the fix away
  3. After fixing line 5, line 6 flagged
  4. Clean lines → the clean all-clear. Plus one ambiguous line to see whether "unsure" ever appears

If it falls short: tighten the prompts first, then swap a model in .env. No rewrite either way.

Simplified on purpose

Plain Unicode math in text inputsinstead of a LaTeX renderer and math editor

STIX Two keeps it looking printed, and students edit simple text. Fractions read as (x+1)/(x−1).

String-matching leak guardinstead of a second AI review call

Deterministic, free, and logged. An AI review plus a retry is for later.

Generic hint on a leakinstead of retrying the check

Your call: a retry API call comes later.

In-memory sessioninstead of storage or a database

The PRD saves nothing between visits.

OpenAI-compatible gatewayGroq now, Gemini by an .env change

Claude would need a small adapter in llm.js.
